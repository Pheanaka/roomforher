I begin my writing with this quote because it captures an important truth. The world must be shaped to support women. While the journey toward equality has come a long way, there is still much more to be done. 


Before delving deeper into this issue, it is worth recalling the history of International Women’s Day.


International Women’s Day is celebrated worldwide on March 8. It commemorates women’s fight for equality and liberation, focusing on gender equality, reproductive rights, and the fight against violence and abuse toward women. 


The day honors the progress that has been made while reminding us of the work still needed to build a more equitable and just world, especially for women and girls.


The first celebration of International Women’s Day dates back to March 19, 1911. More than one million people in Austria, Denmark, Germany and Switzerland gathered to demand women’s rights to vote, fight discrimination and hold public office. 


In 1913, International Women’s Day was adopted in Russia. Women in St. Petersburg went on strike to protest the First World War, a movement that later became a factor leading to the Russian Revolution. Following these protests, Russian women gained the right to vote, and March 8 was eventually recognized as a non-working holiday.


Today, women in many parts of the world, including Cambodia, continue to fight for these same rights. 


The demand for women’s rights has not diminished. Instead, it has grown, with women around the world advocating for equal rights, equal pay and equal opportunities. 


By 1922, International Women’s Day had spread to China, and in 1975 it was officially recognized by the United Nations. Since then, Women’s Day has been celebrated every year on March 8, with Cambodia also observing the occasion.


This year marks the 115th anniversary of International Women’s Day. It is only right to shine light on impactful Cambodian women who have paved the way for young girls. 


For instance, Apsara Sakbun, a Cambodian-Jamaican swimmer, broke the record for Cambodia in the Olympics, becoming the fourth athlete to ever appear in the 50-meter freestyle. Another athlete, Yon Yoeurn, helped the national team reach the 2023 SEA Games semi-finals and secured a runner-up spot in the 2024 AFF Women's Cup. Additionally, she was the first woman to score a goal in the AFC Women’s Championship League.


Cambodia has certainly come a long way. In the past, society often believed that women should not occupy such positions. Today, women are increasingly encouraged to participate in education, sports, and leadership. Although some mindsets still need to change, we must acknowledge both our shortcomings and our responsibility to support women and girls across the country.


While the world is gradually moving toward greater equality, many women and girls in rural areas remain unable to experience that progress. Their opportunities are still limited, as some communities continue to view girls’ education as unnecessary. Gender should never be a determining factor when discussing rights or opportunities.


We must raise awareness for women and girls whose voices remain unheard. Only through collective effort can women’s rights be fully protected and implemented. 


International Women’s Day not only celebrates past achievements but also serves as a powerful reminder of the challenges that still demand immediate and lasting change.


Women are pillars of society. They have the power to transform communities and empower others. There is nothing women cannot achieve. The only barriers are those imposed by society.


If you are a woman reading this, I hope you recognize the rights you hold and the strength you possess. Do not let society discourage you or define your limits. Do not allow misogynistic beliefs to hold you back from pursuing your goals.

And if you are a man reading this, remember that you also play a role in building a better world for women — for your mother, your sister, your relatives and your friends.
